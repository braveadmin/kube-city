Making a big cluster readable

Tested on a recording of a big real cluster: 890 pods in 27 namespaces, replayed identically for every option. Each image is what kube-city actually draws, at the pixel size of a 200×55 terminal unless noted.

Recommendation

  1. Keep the information changes (landmarks, less noise, health map, focus). They fix most of the confusion, in any terminal, at no cost.
  2. Keep half blocks as the default output. It runs at 20 frames per second for about 9% of one CPU core.
  3. Drop Sixel. Tested in your setup: Sixel images do not get from WSL to Windows Terminal, not even a single still image, so no high-resolution mode is possible there.
  4. No pre-rendered sprites. They only pay off with more pixels than half blocks give, which in your setup would mean leaving the terminal for a graphical window.

Where each option leads

Recommended

Half blocks plus information design

  • Works in every terminal, over SSH and in tmux.
  • Map and focus answer "is my cluster healthy?" at any size.
  • The roadmap continues as planned, with code-drawn art.
Ruled out

Sixel

  • Would have given nine times the detail at 3×, at up to about 6 frames per second.
  • Sixel images sent from WSL never reach Windows Terminal, so it cannot work in your setup.
  • Removed from kube-city along with its two extra dependencies.
Not recommended now

Pre-rendered sprites, or a graphical window

  • Sprites need an asset pipeline per theme and only pay off at full resolution, where Sixel is too slow for animation.
  • A native window or browser view gives the best visuals and mouse interaction, but kube-city stops being a terminal companion.
